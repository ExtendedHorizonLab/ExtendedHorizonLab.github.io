Session 1 · From Cases to Recurring Collaboration Tensions
Framing and Cases
Introduction and Lessons from AIED 2026
Introduce the workshop goals and lessons from AIED 2026. Each accepted position paper then receives approximately two minutes to introduce its context, collaboration experience or need, and a question for discussion. Contributions are organized into three or four thematic batches based on recurring collaboration questions rather than discipline.
Identify Tensions
Comparing Cases Across Thematic Batches
After each batch, facilitators help participants compare cases to surface recurring differences in goals, assumptions, methods, evidence, expertise, and stakeholder involvement. Where do assumptions or goals differ across communities? What expertise or stakeholder perspectives are missing? Where do methods or standards of evidence conflict?
Outcome: a small set of shared collaboration tensions.

During the conference break, organizers will finalize these tensions and group assignments, using participants' stated interests and expertise to ensure complementary perspectives within each group.

Session 2 · From Tensions to Ways Forward
Work Through Tensions
Mixed-Group Collaboration Maps
Approximately six to eight mixed groups each examine one tension from Session 1 — for example, an AI researcher, HCI researcher, learning scientist, and educational practitioner examining the same tension from different perspectives. A facilitator guides each group in examining why the tension arises, which perspectives and stakeholders are involved, and what practices, resources, or infrastructures might help collaborators navigate it.
Outcome: a collaboration map.
Cross-Group Synthesis
Comparing Collaboration Maps
Groups compare their maps to identify recurring practices, disagreements, open questions, and opportunities for continued collaboration.
Outcome: a shared synthesis and next steps.
Collaboration Map

Each group documents its discussion using a shared template that captures: (1) the central tension and its context; (2) relevant perspectives, expertise, and stakeholders; (3) underlying differences or unresolved questions; and (4) promising practices, resources, or future opportunities. These materials will inform the post-workshop synthesis.

After the Workshop

Within four weeks after the workshop, we will circulate a draft synthesis of collaboration challenges, promising practices, and open questions for participant comment. The synthesis will organize insights around shared problem framing, methods and evidence, stakeholder involvement, and resources for sustaining collaboration. Rather than forcing consensus, it will document where perspectives converge, diverge, or depend on context. A revised version will be posted on this website, which will also serve as a lightweight hub for sharing participant resources and supporting follow-up connections after CHI.

Access and Documentation

We will share materials in accessible digital formats in advance, support paper or digital note-taking, and provide both written and spoken ways to contribute. Organizers will ask about access requirements ahead of time and coordinate requests with CHI. Discussions will not be recorded by default, and we will seek permission before attributing statements or sharing identifiable examples.
