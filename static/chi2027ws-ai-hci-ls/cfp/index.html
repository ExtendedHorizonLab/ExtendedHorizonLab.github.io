Call for Papers

HAIL Workshop @ CHI 2027

Overview

Human–AI systems for learning increasingly require collaboration among communities with different goals, methods, and forms of evidence.

HAIL brings together researchers and practitioners from AI, HCI, and the learning sciences, along with educators, designers, students, and other educational stakeholders, to examine how such collaborations can be formed, negotiated, and sustained. We invite accessible, non-anonymous position papers of one to two pages, excluding references. Submissions should address collaboration across AI, HCI, and the learning sciences in research on human–AI interaction for learning, including educational practitioners and other stakeholders. Authors should identify the communities and stakeholders involved and explain what their case or perspective reveals about interdisciplinary collaboration.

Submission Directions

Submissions may contribute through one or more of the following directions:

Collaboration Experiences and Lessons

Describe a concrete interdisciplinary collaboration, including who participated, how decisions were negotiated across communities, what worked or proved difficult, and what others might learn. Unsuccessful and unfinished collaborations are welcome.

Collaboration Questions and Needs

Introduce a research or practice problem that would benefit from cross-community collaboration. Explain why collaboration is needed, what expertise is available or missing, and what knowledge, methods, resources, or partnerships would help move the work forward. Prior collaboration experience is not required.

Perspectives from Educational Practice

Reflect on how educators, learners, or other educational stakeholders participate in interdisciplinary collaborations. Contributions may discuss practical needs, barriers, opportunities, or ways stakeholders can shape research questions, technologies, methods, and interpretations.

What to Include

Each submission should identify:

  • A concrete experience, challenge, or problem
  • Its implications for interdisciplinary collaboration
  • One or more questions for workshop discussion

New empirical results are optional.

Guiding Questions

  • How can collaborators develop shared research problems across AI, HCI, and the learning sciences?
  • How can collaborators integrate methods and forms of evidence across communities?
  • How can educational practitioners and learners meaningfully shape interdisciplinary research?
  • What enables interdisciplinary collaborations to persist beyond individual projects?

Formatting

Position papers should be one to two pages, excluding references.

This workshop uses a non-anonymous review process, so submissions do not need to be anonymized.

Please make your submission accessible (for example, by adding alt text to figures and using a readable document structure).

Template and submission details will be announced here together with the submission form.

Review and Selection

Organizers will review submissions for relevance, specificity, discussion potential, and complementary perspectives. Practitioner knowledge and experience will be considered alongside academic contributions. Selection will consider not only the strength of individual submissions, but also how the overall set of participants can contribute complementary experiences and perspectives to the workshop discussions. We expect to accept 12–16 position papers.

To support participant selection and group formation, an online form will collect participants' disciplinary backgrounds, collaboration experiences, expertise they can contribute, and challenges or questions they hope to discuss.

Participation

We aim to welcome approximately 40–60 participants, including researchers, designers, teachers, students, and educational practitioners. Accepted-paper authors will receive priority in accordance with CHI procedures, while the workshop will remain open to other conference attendees. Contributors of accepted papers will provide a brief introduction, a discussion question, and grouping interests ahead of the workshop.

Visibility

With authors' permission, accepted position papers will be posted on the workshop website before the workshop as non-archival materials. After the workshop, a participant-informed synthesis of collaboration challenges, promising practices, and open questions will also be shared here.
