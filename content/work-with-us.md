We’re always looking for self-motivated students to join the lab as PhD students or research interns. The Extended Horizon Lab studies how extended reality and human-centered AI can support learning, collaboration, and social connection. If you’re interested in working with us, take a look at our [publications](/publications/) to see what kinds of questions we ask.

## Prospective PhD students

The [Extended Horizon Lab PhD Student Guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1c3yT4UM0niOylnzBuLVnwC3JqKBMy-PCk_5J_QLPMKk/edit) describes how [Qiao (Georgie) Jin](http://georgiejin.com/) advises students and what she expects from them. Apply through the [NC State Computer Science PhD program](https://csc.ncsu.edu/academics/graduate/phd/) and explain in your statement why you want to work with Georgie and what you hope to study. After submitting your application, you’re welcome to email Georgie with a brief introduction and a description of your research interests. The department’s admissions committee reviews applications first.

## NC State undergraduate and master’s students

You’re welcome to email Georgie any time during the semester if you’re interested in getting involved in research. Tell her a little about yourself, what you’d like to explore, and how much time you can commit. Include a résumé or CV if you have one handy. A relevant course project or work sample is welcome.

If you reach out at the start of the semester, Georgie can invite you to the lab’s project pitches, which usually happen in week two or three. You’ll hear what teams are working on and can follow up about any projects that catch your interest. If you miss the pitches, you can still reach out to ask what projects might be a good fit. The NC State Computer Science research lightning talks are also a good way to learn about projects across the department.

## Postdocs and visitors

If you’re interested in visiting or collaborating, email Georgie with your CV, the research you’d like to pursue together, and your proposed dates. Please mention any funding you already have or plan to apply for.

## FAQ

### What’s the lab culture like?

We want PhD students to grow into independent researchers who can shape their own questions, justify their choices, and carry a project forward. You’ll have people to think with along the way. We share work in progress, give each other constructive feedback, and ask for help early. We value collaboration and take responsibility for the quality of our work. If we find a mistake, we raise it, figure out what happened, and correct it together.

### What does a typical week in the lab look like?

We have a one-hour lab meeting each week, with the option to join remotely. Members also meet in person with their project mentor for about 30 minutes each week. PhD students take part in a hybrid journal club every other week. Between meetings, your time depends on your project. You might read papers, prototype an interface, prepare a study, or work through data with a collaborator. There are also weekly office hours for questions and informal check-ins. The [EH Lab Student Guide for Onboarding](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1LP4L_kMLXbFan7jz7vB5MZGY4kxt579xiW56WWfQiHg/edit) covers the practical details once you join.

### How are PhD students funded?

PhD funding comes from a combination of research assistantships (RAs), teaching assistantships (TAs), and fellowships. The mix can vary by student and semester. If we’re discussing whether you might join the lab, feel free to ask Georgie what funding could look like for you and which opportunities are worth exploring.

### What do you look for in prospective PhD students?

We look for curiosity about people and technology, initiative, and a willingness to revise your ideas when the evidence changes. In your application, tell us about a question you chose to investigate, how you approached it, and what you learned. You can draw on a class project, research, work experience, or something you built on your own. We’re interested in how you think and what you’d like to learn next.

### How can I tell whether the lab is a good fit for me?

Start with a few of our [publications](/publications/) that genuinely interest you and take a look at the PhD Student Guide above. What questions would you want to ask next? What kind of mentoring helps you do your best work? If you have a chance to talk with Georgie or current lab members, ask about how projects get started, what feedback looks like, and how students develop their own research directions.
